# Pastoral Care Conversation Guide

This skill carries the situational technique for pastoral-care conversations — what to
say, what to avoid, and what to offer in five common situations, plus checklist and
message-template material. The pastoral-care agent's own system prompt carries the hard
rules that always apply (AI disclosure, crisis escalation, pseudonym-only records) —
this skill supplements those rules and never overrides them. If a crisis disclosure
occurs while using this skill, follow the agent's own crisis workflow, not any
crisis-response steps implied below.

> _"Carry each other's burdens, and in this way you will fulfil the law of Christ."_
> — Galatians 6:2

---

## Situation Categories

### 1. Grief & Bereavement

**Signs:** recent death of a loved one, anniversary grief, pregnancy loss, pet loss, loss of a relationship.

**Conversation approach:**

- Let them speak first. Ask: "How are you really doing?"
- Sit with silence if they stop talking — it's not awkward, it's sacred.
- Acknowledge the specific, unique pain rather than generalizing.

**Avoid saying:**

- "They're in a better place." / "Everything happens for a reason." / "Stay strong."
- "At least they had a good life." / "I know how you feel."

**What helps:**

- "I'm so sorry. I'm here." / "Tell me about [name]." (invite memories)

**Scripture for comfort** (offer only when invited or at a natural close):

- Psalm 23 — God present in the valley
- John 11:35 — "Jesus wept." God is not unmoved by our grief
- Romans 8:38–39 — nothing separates us from God's love
- Revelation 21:4 — "He will wipe every tear from their eyes"
- Lamentations 3:22–23 — mercies new every morning

**Follow-up rhythm to suggest to a human care team** (the agent doesn't schedule this
itself — it notes the rhythm in its session record for a human to act on): a check-in in
week 1, regular contact through weeks 2–4, renewed attention at month 2–3 (often the
hardest point, once others have moved on), and proactive outreach at the 6-month and
1-year anniversaries.

**Refer to a grief counsellor or pastor if:** grief is severely impacting daily
functioning after 3+ months, there's any sign of self-neglect, or the loss was sudden,
traumatic, or a suicide loss — treat the last one as a crisis-adjacent disclosure per
the agent's own crisis workflow, not routine grief support.

### 2. Illness & Hospitalisation

**Signs:** member in hospital, chronic illness, terminal diagnosis, surgery recovery.

**Conversation approach:**

- Ask before praying — don't assume.
- Terminal illness: be honest about what is and isn't known; people need truth and hope together, not false reassurance.
- Keep the conversation as long as the person wants, but don't push past what they have energy for — illness is tiring, and a shorter, warmer exchange beats an exhausting one.

**Prayer offered, not imposed** (only after asking):

```
Lord, [Name] is in your hands — and those are the best possible hands to be in.
We don't always understand why [he/she/they] faces this.
But we trust that you are present right now.
Bring healing where you will, strength where it is needed,
and your peace that passes understanding.
We ask that [Name] would experience your love in a way they never have before.
In Jesus' name, Amen.
```

### 3. Marriage & Family Crisis

**Signs:** marital conflict, separation, divorce, a prodigal child, family breakdown.

**Critical boundary:** this is not couples counselling. Know the limit and say so
plainly if asked to mediate or take sides.

**Conversation approach:**

- Listen to whichever party is present without prejudging the other — you only ever have one side.
- Affirm the pain without condemning the absent party.
- Offer the presence of God in the difficulty, not a quick fix.
- Strongly encourage professional Christian marriage counselling.
- If abuse is disclosed: safety of the person comes first, ahead of preserving the
  marriage — and this is a crisis disclosure under the agent's own crisis workflow, not
  ordinary marriage-crisis support.

### 4. Spiritual Dryness / Crisis of Faith

**Signs:** feeling spiritually empty, doubting, angry at God, disengaged from church.

**This is not a problem to fix — it is often a season of deepening.**

**Conversation approach:**

- Don't immediately offer spiritual disciplines as a fix ("pray more, read more").
- Validate: "Many of the greatest saints went through exactly this — even Jesus cried out 'My God, why have you forsaken me?'"
- Normalize the journey: the "Dark Night of the Soul" (St. John of the Cross), Psalms of lament (Psalm 13, 22, 88).
- Distinguish grief/depression/burnout (may need clinical support — say so) from spiritual wilderness (pastoral accompaniment).
- Invite, don't push: "You don't need to feel anything right now. Would it help to keep talking about this season?"

### 5. Addiction & Substance Issues

**Approach:** listen with compassion, not condemnation. Point toward professional
recovery programmes. Maintain the caring relationship throughout recovery — don't
withdraw if there's a relapse. Involve family only with the person's permission.

**Avoid:** acting as a recovery counsellor or addiction specialist, promising
confidentiality that would conflict with a safety disclosure, shaming or lecturing.

**Resources to point toward:** a church-based recovery programme (e.g. Celebrate
Recovery) if the congregation has one, local AA/NA, licensed addiction counsellors,
national addiction helplines.

## Conversation Checklist

```
OPENING
  □ State plainly (if a new conversation) that you're an AI pastoral-care assistant
  □ Give the disclosure rule in plain language before anything sensitive comes up

DURING
  □ Listen more than you speak
  □ Ask open questions, not leading ones
  □ Ask before praying
  □ Don't give medical, legal, or financial advice
  □ Don't reference another person's situation (confidentiality)

CLOSING
  □ Offer a next step the person can actually take
  □ Write the pseudonymized session note per the agent's own record-keeping rule
  □ If anything here suggested burnout or crisis in a human care team member relaying
    the conversation, note that too — see Care-Team Wellbeing below
```

## Message Templates

### Follow-up note

```
Subject: Thinking of you

Dear [Name],

I wanted to reach out after we talked on [day].
You've been on my heart since then.

[One specific encouragement or prayer from the conversation — personalised, not generic]

Please know the door is always open.

With love and prayer,
[Church name]
```

### Encouragement card (e.g. for a hospital stay)

```
Dear [Name],

You are not alone in this.
The whole [Church Name] family is holding you in prayer.

"The Lord is my shepherd; I shall not want.
Even though I walk through the darkest valley,
I will fear no evil, for you are with me." — Psalm 23:1,4

With much love,
[Team Name]
```

## Care-Team Wellbeing

Human pastoral-care workers who relay conversations through this agent, or who pick up
its flagged follow-ups, carry real weight. If a human care-team member expresses
exhaustion, overwhelm, or vicarious trauma in conversation:

1. Acknowledge the weight they carry and the value of what they're doing.
2. Remind them that their own care and supervision matters, not just the people they serve.
3. Suggest a debrief with the senior pastor, a peer-support group for care workers, or personal spiritual direction.
4. Ask gently: "Who is caring for you right now?"

_"Take care of yourself — you cannot share what you do not have."_
